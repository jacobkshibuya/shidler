## Executive Summary (≤150 words)

### Overview  
Our firm expects to receive **€8,000,000 in one year** from a European pharmaceutical distributor. The current **EUR/USD spot rate** is approximately **1.09**, and the **one-year forward rate** is **1.0890**. This creates a **foreign exchange (FX) receivable exposure**, meaning the USD value we ultimately receive will depend on how the euro performs against the dollar over the next 12 months.

---
## Background

### FX Exposure and Risk  
If the **euro depreciates** against the dollar (e.g., EUR/USD falls below 1.09), our **USD proceeds** will decline, reducing the value of our receivable. For example, a **5% drop** in the euro’s value could lower our dollar receipts by nearly **$400,000**. Conversely, if the euro strengthens, our USD proceeds increase — but this potential gain is uncertain and cannot be relied upon for accurate budgeting or financial reporting.  

FX volatility therefore introduces **earnings and cash flow risk**, making it difficult to forecast operating results with confidence.

---
## Findings (figures/tables referenced)
#### 1. Forward Contract  
- **Pros:** Locks in a fixed USD amount today; simple execution; no upfront cost.  
- **Cons:** No participation in favorable FX movements; binding obligation to settle.  

#### 2. Money Market Hedge  
- **Pros:** Uses borrowing and lending to synthetically fix exchange exposure; verifies interest rate parity; flexible for firms with strong credit lines.  
- **Cons:** Operationally complex; may tie up capital or borrowing capacity.  

#### 3. Options Hedge (Put or Call on EUR)  
- **Pros:** Provides downside protection while preserving upside if the euro appreciates.  
- **Cons:** Requires upfront premium; more complex pricing and management.
